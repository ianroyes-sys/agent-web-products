## 1. The insight that determines who we call first

> **Your first customer is usually the person already selling into the niche, not the end business.**

This is the single highest-leverage idea in the whole plan, and it is counterintuitive enough that it gets ignored.

The obvious move with a Huntsville GovCon dataset is to call Huntsville GovCon firms. It is the wrong first move, for four reasons:

1. **They have no budget line for it.** A 30-person sub does not have a "market intelligence" line item. Creating a budget line is a longer sale than filling one.
2. **They buy once a year, on a cycle you do not control.** The sale rides on their fiscal planning calendar.
3. **They are slow.** A small defense firm's purchasing process involves the owner, and the owner is on a proposal.
4. **One of them is one customer.** A consultant serving twelve of them is a distribution channel.

The people **already selling into** Huntsville GovCon — consultants, proposal shops, staffing firms, M&A buyers — have the opposite profile. They have an existing intelligence budget, they buy tools continuously because tools are their cost of goods, they decide fast because the buyer is often the whole company, and each one carries us to their entire client base. They also give brutally honest feedback, because they will resell the output and their reputation is attached to it.

**Sequence: sellers first, end firms second, agents third.**

## 2. The five ICPs, in call order

### ICP 1 — Capture managers (at small and mid primes)

The single most qualified individual buyer in the market.

| | |
|---|---|
| **Who** | The person at a $10M–$250M Huntsville firm responsible for identifying, qualifying, and positioning for pursuits before an RFP drops. Title varies: Capture Manager, Director of Business Development, VP Growth, sometimes just the owner at firms under 50 people. |
| **Where they are** | Inside the 107 mid-tier and the larger of the 271 small-tier firms in the dataset. |
| **What they already pay for** | GovWin IQ or an equivalent subscription; SAM.gov (free); agency forecast documents (free, scattered); conference and association dues; sometimes a proposal consultant on retainer. |
| **What keeps them up at night** | Missing a recompete window. A capture cycle on a serious pursuit is 12–18 months. Finding out about an expiring contract 60 days before the RFP means you have already lost it. |
| **What they'd pay us** | $199–299/mo per seat for the Recompete Radar with alerts filtered to their NAICS and agencies. Plus $499–1,500 per one-off Incumbent Vulnerability or Teaming Target report against a named pursuit. |
| **Why they buy from us over GovWin** | Not because we are better across the board — we are not. Because we are *specific to their metro*, priced for a firm their size, and the recompete view is the front door rather than a feature buried three clicks deep in an enterprise suite. |

**The pitch:**
> "There are 1,463 Huntsville contracts expiring in the next 24 months worth $22.7B. 492 of them — $5.85B — expire inside 90 days. I can show you the ones in your NAICS codes, with the incumbent and the exact date, in about four minutes. If none of them are relevant to you, I will go away and you have lost four minutes."

### ICP 2 — Proposal shops and GovCon consultants

The highest-leverage buyer. One sale, many downstream users.

| | |
|---|---|
| **Who** | Independent consultants and small firms who write proposals, run capture, or do BD-as-a-service for Huntsville contractors. Frequently retired government or ex-prime, often 1–10 people, often carrying 5–20 clients. |
| **What they already pay for** | Research subscriptions, Shipley or APMP training and materials, proposal software, virtual assistants, and a great deal of their own unbilled research time. |
| **Their actual pain** | Unbilled hours. Every hour spent assembling a market picture for a client pitch is an hour they cannot invoice. They are not buying data; they are buying back their weekends. |
| **What they'd pay us** | $299–599/mo for a multi-client seat with export rights. Plus a white-label or co-branded arrangement — they put their logo on the Recompete Radar and hand it to clients as their own deliverable. That is worth a premium and it is the fastest path to distribution. |
| **Why this ICP is first on the call list** | A consultant with twelve clients who adopts our data introduces the product to twelve firms without us making twelve calls. They are also the best possible source of truth on whether the ten outputs in `01-ARSENAL-INDEX-PLAYBOOK.md` are the right ten. |

**The pitch:**
> "You already build a market picture for every client pitch, and you do not bill for those hours. I have Huntsville prime awards refined into 426 firm profiles and 1,463 dated recompetes — incumbent, value, agency, NAICS, expiry. Put your logo on it. It becomes a deliverable instead of overhead."

### ICP 3 — GovCon BD consultants and fractional BD

Adjacent to ICP 2 but a distinct motion: they sell *pipeline*, not documents.

| | |
|---|---|
| **Who** | Fractional BD leaders, growth advisors, and boutique firms retained to build a pipeline rather than write a specific proposal. |
| **What they already pay for** | Data subscriptions, lead lists, CRM, event attendance, and their own network. |
| **Their pain** | They must show pipeline in the first 30 days of an engagement or the retainer does not renew. Every one of them has a "prove value fast" problem in month one. |
| **What they'd pay us** | $299–599/mo, and they are the most likely of all five to want API access so recompete data flows straight into their CRM. First real candidate for per-call pricing. |
| **The hook that lands** | We do not sell them a database. We sell them **a defensible first-30-days deliverable**: a named, dated, dollar-valued target list for their new client, generated in an afternoon. |

**The pitch:**
> "Your first 30 days on a new retainer have to produce a target list that survives scrutiny. Here is that list, prebuilt, for any Huntsville client you sign: every expiring contract in their space with the incumbent, the value, the agency, and the date. 541715 alone has 250 contracts worth $5.37B in recompete."

### ICP 4 — Staffing firms recruiting cleared talent

The least obvious ICP and possibly the most underserved.

| | |
|---|---|
| **Who** | Recruiting and staffing firms placing cleared engineers, program managers, and systems folks into Huntsville defense work. |
| **What they already pay for** | LinkedIn Recruiter, job boards, ATS, clearance-focused sourcing tools. |
| **The insight they are missing** | **A contract award is a hiring event, and a contract expiry is a hiring event in the opposite direction.** A firm that just won a $180M Army contract is about to staff it. A firm whose $107M contract expires in eight months is about to have people looking. Nobody is selling them that timing signal in this metro. |
| **What they'd pay us** | $199–399/mo for expiry and new-award alerts filtered to firms and NAICS they recruit into. |
| **Why this is a good early ICP** | Their sales cycle is short, they are used to paying monthly for data tools, and the value is obvious in one sentence — no education required. |

**The pitch:**
> "IronMountain Solutions has a $180.7M Army contract expiring 2026-09-07. Beyond New Horizons has $910.2M expiring 2026-09-30. Those are staffing events on both sides — the incumbent's people start looking, and the winner staffs up fast. I track 1,463 of these with dates, for every firm headquartered in Madison County."

### ICP 5 — M&A and PE buyers scouting Huntsville targets

Lowest volume, highest single-deal value, longest cycle.

| | |
|---|---|
| **Who** | Lower-middle-market PE funds, family offices, and corp-dev teams at larger primes, looking to acquire Huntsville GovCon firms. Defense services is an active roll-up category. |
| **What they already pay for** | Bankers, diligence firms, data rooms, industry research — in tens of thousands of dollars per look. |
| **What they need that we have** | Two things. (a) **Screening** — which of the 426 firms fit a size and capability thesis. (b) **Diligence** — for a specific target: revenue concentration, expiry exposure, agency dependency, award velocity. A target with 100% of its book expiring in 18 months is a materially different asset than one with 15% expiring, and that difference is directly computable from our data. |
| **What they'd pay us** | $2,500–10,000 per screening engagement or per-target diligence pack. Possibly an annual license at $10–25K for a fund actively working the metro. Priced against banker and diligence fees, not against SaaS. |
| **Reality check** | This is a relationship sale into a market where a college student with no track record starts at a disadvantage. It is worth two exploratory calls in the first 90 days, not a campaign. Treat it as an option, not a plan. |

**The pitch:**
> "Before you pay for diligence on a Huntsville target, here is what public award data says about them: agency concentration, award velocity, and exactly how much of their book expires inside 24 months. In this market 104 firms above $10M draw more than 90% of their revenue from a single sub-agency. That is the risk that does not show up in an add-back schedule."

### The end firms — second, not first

Once one to three of the above are paying, the 426 firms themselves become the volume market. They are already segmented in the dataset:

| Tier | Count | Threshold | Offer |
|---|---:|---|---|
| Major | 48 | ≥$250M | Not a subscription buyer — already has enterprise tools. Sell them *Teaming Target Lists* for small-business subcontracting plans, and sell them Legible. |
| Mid | 107 | $25M–$250M | The core subscription market. $199–299/mo. 253 of the 426 have at least one contract expiring; those are the warm list. |
| Small | 271 | <$25M | Free tier and one-off reports. Some will convert; most are content audience and referral sources. |

The best-qualified single list to work: the **253 firms with at least one expiring contract**. Every one of them has a dated, specific, verifiable reason to take the call. The 173 with nothing expiring can wait.

**Pricing notes.**
Annual is priced at ten months to pull cash forward and cut churn management, which matters when the operator has 15–20 hours a week. Do not discount below these numbers to win an early logo — discount by *adding* a one-off report instead, so the headline price stays intact for the next buyer. The first three customers can and should get a founding-customer rate in exchange for a testimonial and a monthly feedback call; state the discount as time-limited in writing.

## 4. Legible pricing ladder

| Offer | Price | Scope |
|---|---|---|
| **Free snapshot** | $0 | One question, one company, screenshot of what AI says today. The entire sales motion. |
| **Audit — small firm** | $3,000 | Up to 10 questions across 3 assistants, findings, fix list, delivered as a document plus a 45-minute call |
| **Audit — standard** | $5,000–7,500 | Adds competitor comparison, structured-data recommendations, `/llms.txt` draft, and content gap map |
| **Audit — large firm** | $10,000–20,000 | Multi-division, multi-capability, stakeholder readout, prioritized remediation roadmap |
| **Remediation** | Quoted, typically $2,500–10,000 | Actually implementing the fixes: structured data, `/llms.txt`, capability pages, entity clarification |
| **Monitoring** | $500–1,500/mo | Same questions re-run monthly, movement report, alert on material change. 6- or 12-month term. |

The recurring line is the business; the audit is customer acquisition that happens to be profitable. Target attach rate from audit to monitoring: at least 50%. If it is materially lower, the audit is not proving that the problem persists, which means the audit is written wrong.

**Blocker:** these are invoiced engagements. Invoicing requires the entity and EIN, and collecting by card requires live Stripe. Neither exists today. Until they do, Legible can be sold and scheduled but not billed.

## 6. Agent pay-per-lookup — the pricing rationale

The prices already implemented in `apps/arsenal-index/src/lib/paywall.ts`:

| Resource | Price | Unit |
|---|---:|---|
| `recompetes.full` | $0.02 | per call |
| `firms.detail` | $0.01 | per call |
| `export.bulk` | $2.50 | per export |

### Why fractions of a cent to a few cents

**A machine does not experience price friction.** A human deciding on a $0.02 charge burns more than two cents of attention on the decision itself — that is the whole reason micropayments failed for thirty years. An agent under a budget performs a comparison: is the expected value of this answer greater than $0.02? For a recompete lookup inside a capture workflow, it is greater by five or six orders of magnitude. It pays and moves on. There is no cart abandonment, no sticker shock, no "let me think about it."

**Price for call volume, not per-answer value.** The temptation is to reason "this answer could inform a $180M pursuit, so charge $50." That is human-web logic, and it kills the volume that makes the model work. The agent-web logic is the opposite: price low enough that no agent ever routes around you, and let frequency do the work. A resource called 50,000 times a month at $0.02 is $1,000/mo from a single integration, and it got there with no sales call, no contract, and no onboarding.

**Being routed around is the only real failure mode.** If we price at $2.00/call, an agent's planner will try to synthesize the answer from free sources, and it will usually get something 80% correct. At $0.02, synthesizing is not worth the tokens. **Our competitor is not another data vendor; it is the agent deciding to guess.** Price below the cost of guessing.

**Bulk is priced differently on purpose.** `export.bulk` at $2.50 is not a volume discount on individual calls — it is a distinct product for a distinct intent. Per-call is a workflow; bulk export is someone building their own copy. Priced so that bulk-exporting the whole dataset repeatedly costs more than subscribing, which is the correct incentive.

**The pricing must be discoverable.** The 402 response body states the price, the currency, the unit, and the accepted schemes in machine-readable form. An agent that cannot parse the price will treat the resource as unavailable. Undocumented pricing is functionally identical to being offline.

### Realistic expectations

Per-call revenue in the first 12 months will be small and possibly zero. Agent-initiated commercial traffic to a niche resource is early. The reasons to ship it anyway:

1. It costs almost nothing — the handshake is already written.
2. The request log is a demand signal available nowhere else. Who asks, how often, for what.
3. Being priced and machine-payable before the demand arrives is what makes us the default when it does.
4. It is the most credible possible proof that RMG actually understands the agent internet, which matters for every other conversation in this portfolio.

**Blocked on:** live Stripe and the entity. The 402 gate works; settlement does not.

## 7. What is not researched yet

Stated so nobody mistakes an estimate for a finding:

- **GovWin IQ / Deltek pricing is not publicly listed.** It is commonly discussed as a four-to-five-figure annual subscription that scales with seats and modules, but no verified figure is in hand. Do not quote a competitor price in any customer-facing material until it is confirmed from a real quote or a published source.
- **The number of capture managers, proposal consultants, staffing firms, and PE buyers active in Huntsville is not counted.** The 426 firms are counted; the people around them are not. The first 20 named targets in `04-90-DAY-PLAN.md` are a research task, not an existing list.
- **Willingness to pay at every price point above is an estimate**, anchored on what comparable GovCon tools and consulting deliverables cost, not on a single conversation with a Huntsville buyer. The first ten calls exist to correct these numbers. Expect to be wrong in at least one direction.
- **Set-aside type is not in the dataset**, so any claim about small-business set-aside volume is currently inferred from winner size rather than measured.
